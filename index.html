The day my dad was diagnosed, he didn't talk about odds. He sent us an article about this Swedish idea and told us how we were going to do this: by living life with joy and vigor. That's what kärt besvär means to our family — living joyfully, even with a tear in your eye.

Seven months in, that's exactly what he's done — with unwavering bravery, graciousness, and, importantly, humor. This campaign runs on the same fuel.

The hardest diagnosis — and why right now matters

Pancreatic cancer is brutal for two specific, fixable reasons. It's hard to detect — there's no routine screening and symptoms stay vague, so most people are diagnosed after it has already spread. And for more than a decade, treatment options barely moved: for most patients, it has come down to two standard chemotherapy regimens.

But this year, something changed. The first RAS-targeting drug — going after the mutation that drives over 90% of pancreatic cancers, considered "undruggable" for forty years — roughly doubled survival in its phase 3 trial. My dad is waiting to receive it now. More new therapies and real early-detection science are right behind it.

Where every dollar goes

Every dollar goes directly to the University of Pennsylvania, designated for the Penn Pancreatic Cancer Research Center — the team treating my dad, and one of the country's leading centers for pancreatic cancer research. Its director, Dr. Robert Vonderheide, has pioneered immunotherapy approaches for pancreatic cancer now in later-stage clinical trials.

We're directing this campaign toward the two things that change the story of this disease: early detection, so more families catch it when it's most treatable, and novel therapies that push the boundaries of a historically stubborn cancer. Penn's PCRC leads in both.

Donations go straight to Penn, a 501(c)(3) — they're tax-deductible, you'll receive a receipt from Penn, and nothing passes through us.
